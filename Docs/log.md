# Update Log

## 2026-11-16

* **Decision**: Settled the torch fiction as a medium-future compact fusion drive in the spirit of *The Expanse*'s Epstein drive: waste heat is coupled into the reaction mass, the reactor is not simulated, and fuel capacity and consumption are gameplay balance values. Aligned the game vision, design pillars, flight model, ADR-004, and the roadmap risk with that reading.
* **Creation**: Built the headless flight core in `Assets/Scripts/Simulation/`: `ShipState`, a continuous `GravityField` over the on-rails catalogue, an adaptive RK4 `ShipIntegrator` that advances to a Julian date, `TwoBody` helpers, a `Quintic` reference curve, `PlanArc` and `TrajectoryPlan`, `ParkingOrbit`, `SphereOfInfluence`, `TripPlanner`, and `Autopilot`. `SolarSystemModel.ComputeState` returns any body's state at an arbitrary date without mutating the model, so the field can be evaluated at RK4 stage times inside a frame.
* **Decision**: [ADR-005](decisions/adr-005-continuous-gravity.md) — the ship flies through summed point-mass gravity from every catalogue body, pruned below 10⁻¹¹ m/s² and clamped at each surface. No patched-conic frame switches; spheres of influence survive only as planning waypoints.
* **Decision**: [ADR-006](decisions/adr-006-quintic-plan.md) — a Go To plan is a chain of quintic arcs (departure, cruise, capture) matching position, velocity, and acceleration at both ends, with the thrust equal to the curve's acceleration minus local gravity. Exact arrival, deterministic, smooth, and 35–75% slower than the bang-bang ideal.
* **Creation**: Added 46 EditMode tests across `ShipDynamicsTests`, `GravityFieldTests`, `PlanGeometryTests`, `ShipIntegratorTests`, `TripPlannerTests`, and `FlightExecutionTests`, plus a `FlightFixture`. `tools/check.sh` now runs 157 tests. Gateway→Mars plans in 4.44 days at a 9.76 m/s² peak, arrives within 0.01 m and 0.0000 m/s, and holds a 3,898 km Mars orbit at e ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁶ after three orbits.
* **Fix**: the first planner pointed the escape arc at the target's arrival position. When the target was behind the departure body the ship had to chase that body around its orbit, which fed back into the flight time and grew the plan from 4.4 to 17.5 days. The escape now follows the ship's own relative motion, which decouples it from the arrival date.
* **Update**: documented the flight core in the flight model (a "flight model in code" section and measured plan times), navigation and arrival (the implemented plan, parking orbit, deep-well limit), orbital mechanics (the ship's field), architecture (the flight-core type table), testing (the new suites, 157 tests, and the Julian-date precision floor), and the roadmap (milestone 1 done, milestone 2 is the scene, milestone 3 is fuel).
* **Decision**: Jupiter is explicitly out of reach for the single-arc capture. Its parking orbit sits at 19.6 m/s², twice the drive, and the planner reports the plan as infeasible rather than emitting a path the ship cannot fly. Measured on the capture arc alone, the peak thrust plateaus at 19.6 m/s² no matter how long the flight: the geometric term falls as 1/T² but the *floor* is the parking orbit's own gravity, because the curve must arrive with that acceleration while it is still millions of kilometres out. Starting the capture closer in does not help (the endpoint is unchanged), and a spiral-of-arcs capture was prototyped and rejected because radially aligned waypoints force the ship to cancel its tangential velocity at every waypoint. A transfer conic (coast where the thrust is exactly zero) is the recorded next step; as an interim policy, a parking radius of at least √(GM / 0.4 a_max) makes Jupiter feasible at 177,965 km with a 7.95 m/s² peak. *(Superseded by the gravity-ceiling decision below.)*
* **Decision**: Adopted the parking-orbit **gravity ceiling** as the fix for deep wells. `ParkingOrbit.RadiusM` now takes the drive's design acceleration and lifts the arrival radius until `GM / r²` is at most 40% of it: `max(1.15 R, R + 300 km, √(GM / (0.4 a_max)))`, still capped at half the Hill radius. The 40% is a measured margin — the capture arc's best peak sits at the parking gravity, so leaving 60% of the drive free covers the curve's shape term and the tracking feedback. Because the ceiling tracks the drive rather than a fixed radius, it is self-consistent: halving the drive raises the orbit instead of making it unflyable.
* **Update**: Everything else keeps its orbit: Mercury 2,806 km, Mars 3,898 km, the Moon 2,037 km, and all 22 moons were already under the ceiling. Six bodies move out: Earth 7,327 → 9,983 km, Venus 6,960 → 9,012 km, Uranus 29,166 → 38,059 km, Neptune 28,315 → 41,342 km, Saturn 66,967 → 97,380 km, and Jupiter 80,398 → 177,965 km. Two consequences are recorded rather than hidden: Earth arrival is now at 3,611 km altitude, and Saturn's 97,380 km orbit lies inside the ring system the catalogue does not model.
* **Fix**: Jupiter is now reachable end to end. Gateway→Jupiter plans in 10.32 days at a 9.78 m/s² peak and 5,189 km/s of delta-v, arrives within 10.6 m of the 177,965 km orbit, and holds it at an eccentricity of 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ after three orbits. Venus and Saturn also changed: Venus 2.55 → 2.85 days, Saturn 13.96 → 12.88 days, because a higher arrival orbit is easier to fly into but further from the sphere-of-influence entry.
* **Update**: Replaced the "Jupiter is infeasible" planner test with a feasibility test, and added `EveryParkingOrbitSitsUnderTheGravityCeiling` (all 30 planets and moons), `TheCeilingLeavesShallowWellsWhereTheyWere`, `TheCeilingLiftsTheTallWells`, `AStrongerDriveParksLower`, and an end-to-end `FlyToJupiterAndArriveInAStableOrbit`. * **Decision**: Deferred the transfer-conic capture and moved to milestone 2 (scene integration) instead. The gravity ceiling already makes every body reachable, so the conic is a quality improvement — lower, cheaper, Oberth-correct arrivals — rather than a blocker. Recorded the design requirements and expected payoffs in a new "Deferred" section of the roadmap: a `ConicArc` with a state-vector propagator, coasting capture arcs whose commanded thrust is exactly zero, and a periapsis burn plus circularisation.
* **Creation**: Milestone 2, the scene flight. `FlightSession` (plain C#) owns the ship state, the active plan, the autopilot, and the integrator, and advances in legs split at plan arc boundaries so the arrival state is hit exactly. `WarpPolicy` caps time warp by clearance to the nearest surface (25% of the time to reach it), the local orbital timescale (2%), and the time to the next plan boundary (5%), rounded down to the clock's power-of-ten scales. `TrajectoryLine` draws the sampled plan with a solid departure and cruise, a dashed capture using a tiled alpha texture, a flip marker at the along-track thrust reversal, and an arrival marker. `FlightReport` builds the flight panel as pure data, the same pattern as `BodyDetails`. `CameraAttitude` keeps the destination framed with a 40°/s slew. The details screen now offers **Go To** (the flight) and **Jump (debug)** (the old eased teleport); free flight moved behind the backquote key and cancels any plan.
* **Decision**: [ADR-007](decisions/adr-007-flight-director.md) — one owner for the ship state, and an automatic warp cap. `FlightSession` owns the ship; the bootstrap only advances the clock and asks the session to reach it, clamping the clock back when the 2,000-substep frame budget runs out. Free flight is an explicit debug teleport, not a ship. Go To raises the warp to a ~30-real-second trip. The camera frames the destination rather than the burn, because a capture burns retrograde.
* **Fix**: the first frame-loop run arrived 2.5 km and 0.35 m/s off the parking orbit because the clock could ask for a date up to one second past the arrival, so the ship coasted beyond the matched state. The session now stops the ship on the arrival date even when the clock asks for longer, and the bootstrap clamps the clock back to it; the arrival error through the frame loop is 0.0 m and 0.0000 m/s.
* **Creation**: Added `FlightSessionTests`, `WarpPolicyTests`, `PlanPathTests`, `FlightReportTests`, `CameraAttitudeTests`, and `SceneFlightTests`, plus `NextBoundaryJulianDate` and `FindFlipJulianDate` on `TrajectoryPlan`. * **Creation**: Replaced the fixed view with an orbit camera. `OrbitCamera` (plain C#) holds yaw, pitch (±85°), and a distance (2,000 km to 5 billion km) and does the multiplicative zoom, the shortest-turn slew, and the distance easing. `ShipCameraRig` reads right-drag and the wheel, places the camera on the sphere around the render origin, scales the near plane with the orbit (2%, clamped to 200 km–50 km), and pulls the camera out of any body it would enter. A plan, a debug jump, or a reset frames its destination at `clamp(0.6 × ship→target, 3 × radius, 3.3 AU)`; the aim and the distance follow separately, so zooming keeps the planet centred. Added a ten-pixel `ShipView` marker at the origin, pointing along the burn, so the orbit camera has a subject. `ShipController` lost its camera and mouse-look code; `FocusTransition` is position-only; `CameraAttitude` was removed.
* **Decision**: [ADR-008](decisions/adr-008-orbit-camera.md) — keep the ship at the render origin ([ADR-002](decisions/adr-002-double-precision.md)) and orbit the camera around it, rather than a chase offset.
