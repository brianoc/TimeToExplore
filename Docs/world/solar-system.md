# The setting

The game takes place in the real solar system, in the late 21st century. The
planets, moons, and major asteroids are where they actually are, moving on
orbits fitted to real ephemerides. The only invented objects are the six
Lagrange stations.

There are no fictional planets, no warp gates, and no artificial gravity. The
player's ship is the one piece of speculative technology that changes the
practical scale of the map.

The Sun is 109 Earths across. Neptune's orbit is about 30 times Earth's. The
solar system is mostly empty, and the emptiness is the point.

# What moves

Everything except the Sun:

* Planets follow heliocentric Keplerian orbits.
* Moons follow planetocentric orbits, in the planet's equatorial plane where
  that is physically meaningful.
* Asteroids follow heliocentric Keplerian orbits.
* Stations are recomputed each frame from the positions of their two Lagrange
  partners, so they follow the pair rather than a fixed point.

The simulation epoch is 2026-11-15. The world is deterministic: given a Julian
date, every body has exactly one position. There is no random drift and no
runtime physics solver in the ephemeris.

# Physical data

Planet masses are expressed as `GM` (gravitational parameter, m³/s²), which is
what the orbit propagator actually needs. Radii are mean radii. Rotation periods
are sidereal, with negative values for retrograde rotation. Axial tilts are
relative to the orbital plane.

The Moon is a special case: Earth's heliocentric orbit is the Earth–Moon
barycentre orbit from JPL, and the Moon is placed relative to Earth's centre.
The Earth's 4,700 km wobble about the barycentre is not modelled. This is below
the radius of Earth and well below the navigation precision of the game, but it
is a known simplification.

# Why the asteroids matter

The belt is not just scenery. It provides:

* **Navigation hazards and waypoints.** A 300 km asteroid is a real object with
  a real gravity well, small but non-zero.
* **Resource sites.** Water ice, metals, and volatiles are the economic reason
  to go there.
* **Science targets.** A survey contract can be as simple as "go and look".
* **Route texture.** The belt is sparse, but it is not empty; a course through it
  should feel different from a course through clear space.

The 100 objects in the catalogue are the largest by diameter, from Ceres
(939 km) down to about 131 km. The real belt has over a million objects larger
than 1 km; the catalogue is a playable subset, not a complete simulation.

# The stations

The six stations are the only fictional objects. They sit at real Lagrange
points of real pairs:

* Earth–Moon L1 (Gateway Station),
* Sun–Earth L1 and L2 (Helios Watch, Nyx Anchor),
* Sun–Mars L1 (Ares Junction),
* Sun–Jupiter L4 (Jovian Transfer Yard),
* Saturn–Titan L1 (Titan Reach).

They exist to make the map legible and to give the delta-v economy a shape:
depots, repair yards, rescue bases, and markets. See
[Lagrange stations](lagrange-stations.md).
